Heart Tissue: Sexual Dimorphism in Exercise Response

MOTRPAC · DECODE 2026

Problem Statement

Endurance exercise training (ExT) benefits cardiovascular health, but sex-specific molecular mechanisms driving cardiac adaptation remain poorly characterized. MoTrPAC generated the largest public multi-omic resource on this question, profiling 18 solid tissues and blood from male and female rats undergoing endurance training across four time points. Applying this pipeline to adipose tissue revealed profound sexual dimorphism at rest and in the dynamic response to training. No independent replication of this sexual-dimorphism pipeline has been applied to heart tissue, despite matching data being available.

Research Questions

  1. Does sedentary heart tissue show sex-specific differences in gene expression and enriched pathways, similar to adipose tissue?
  2. How does the cardiac transcriptomic/proteomic response to ExT differ between males and females across the training time course?
  3. Do the sex-specific pathways identified overlap with known cardiac disease pathways (e.g., hypertrophic/dilated cardiomyopathy, mitochondrial dysfunction)?

Key Findings

Across every omic layer, baseline sex differences dominate over training effects — the same pattern reported in scWAT.

LayerSedentary M vs. F
Transcripts (RNA-seq)870 significant (FDR < 0.05) · 575 higher in males, 295 higher in females
GO pathways (FGSEA)127 significant · male: OXPHOS, ATP metabolism · female: RNA splicing, methylation
WGCNA co-expression modules2 of 11 significant · T1/turquoise ↔ female (ρ = −0.87), T2/blue ↔ male (ρ = 0.87)
Proteins (global proteomics)1,249 significant · 633 higher in males, 616 higher in females

At 8 weeks, male–female concordance is weak (ρ = 0.221 transcripts, ρ = 0.24 proteins) — female hearts trend mitochondrial / energetic (OXPHOS, TCA cycle, ATP synthesis), while male hearts trend structural / vascular (aortic/vascular development, VEGF & PI3K signaling).
